# 📊 Employment Data Comprehensive Analysis Summary

**Dataset:** employment-data.csv  
**Source:** Business Data Collection - BDC (New Zealand)  
**Analysis Date:** November 16, 2025  
**Records Analyzed:** 20,108

---

## 🎯 Executive Summary

This analysis examines **14 years of New Zealand employment data** (2011-2025) covering:
- **Filled jobs** across industries, regions, demographics
- **Total earnings** by sector and demographic groups
- **Quarterly time series** with actual, seasonally adjusted, and trend data

### Key Highlights

✅ **Overall Growth:** Employment increased significantly across most sectors  
✅ **Service Sector Boom:** Health Care (+169.5%), Education (+79.5%), Other Services (+121.9%)  
✅ **Regional Leader:** Auckland dominates with 769,959 jobs (latest period)  
✅ **Gender Balance:** Both male and female employment growing steadily  
✅ **Data Quality:** 91.5% complete with consistent quarterly reporting

### 2. Regional Employment Distribution

**Top 5 Employment Centers (2025.06):**

1. **Auckland:** 769,959 jobs (34% of analyzed regions)
2. **Canterbury:** 302,898 jobs
3. **Wellington:** 246,835 jobs
4. **Waikato:** 219,360 jobs
5. **Christchurch City:** 186,995 jobs

**Key Insight:** Auckland alone accounts for more employment than the next 3 regions combined, highlighting significant regional concentration.

### 3. Gender Employment Trends

| Gender | 2011 Jobs | 2025 Jobs | Growth | % Change |
|--------|-----------|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Male** | 861,368 | 1,124,807 | +263,439 | +30.6% |
| **Female** | 865,507 | 1,134,933 | +269,426 | +31.1% |

**Key Insight:** Nearly equal employment growth between genders, with female employment slightly outpacing male employment.

### 4. Age Demographics

**Employment by Age Group (Latest Period):**

- **Peak Employment:** 25-29 age group (highest employment numbers)
- **Youth Employment:** 15-19 age group shows volatility but recent decline
- **Mature Workers:** 50+ age groups maintaining stable employment

**Trend:** Shift toward older workforce as population ages, with 30-44 age groups showing strongest stability.

### 5. Seasonal Patterns

**Agriculture, Forestry & Fishing:**
- **Q1 (Mar):** Peak season - 98,720 avg jobs
- **Q2 (Jun):** Low season - 89,094 avg jobs  
- **Q3 (Sep):** Low season - 86,748 avg jobs
- **Q4 (Dec):** Peak season - 97,347 avg jobs

**Pattern:** Clear seasonal cycle with 12-14% variation between peak and low seasons.

**Manufacturing:**
- More stable across quarters
- Slight Q1 peak (221,407 jobs)
- Q3 dip (213,678 jobs)

### 6. Earnings Analysis

**Total Earnings Growth (in millions):**

| Industry | 2011 Earnings | 2025 Earnings | Growth |
|----------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| **Total Industry** | $20,722M | $45,103M | +117.7% |
| **Health Care** | $2,218M | $5,976M | +169.5% |
| **Education** | $2,194M | $3,939M | +79.5% |

**Key Insight:** Earnings growth outpacing employment growth in service sectors, indicating wage increases and productivity gains.

## 💡 Strategic Insights

### Economic Transformation

1. **Service Economy Shift:** Dramatic growth in health care, education, and professional services indicates New Zealand's transition to a service-based economy.

2. **Aging Population Impact:** Strong growth in health care sector (+169.5%) reflects demographic changes and increased demand for health services.

3. **Manufacturing Stability:** Despite global trends, manufacturing maintains steady employment with modest growth (+7.6%).

### Regional Dynamics

1. **Urban Concentration:** Major cities (Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch) dominate employment landscape.

2. **Regional Resilience:** Smaller regions maintaining stable employment despite urbanization trends.

3. **Economic Hubs:** Canterbury and Waikato emerging as significant employment centers outside Auckland.

### Workforce Composition

1. **Gender Parity:** Near-equal employment between males and females with balanced growth rates.

2. **Age Distribution:** Prime working age (25-44) represents largest employment segment.

3. **Youth Employment:** Declining trend in 15-19 age group may reflect increased education participation.

### Seasonal Dynamics

1. **Agriculture Volatility:** 12-14% seasonal variation requires workforce flexibility.

2. **Service Stability:** Most service sectors show minimal seasonal variation.

3. **Planning Implications:** Businesses need to account for predictable quarterly patterns.
